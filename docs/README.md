# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository centralizes OctoAcme's project management knowledge and provides a practical guide to how the organization plans, delivers, communicates, and improves work.

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured project lifecycle that begins with project initiation, continues through planning and execution, and closes with release and continuous improvement. The goal is to make project work consistent, scalable, and transparent across teams while reducing reliance on undocumented tribal knowledge.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme starts every initiative with a clear definition of the problem, business need, stakeholders, and success metrics. During initiation, the team documents a one-pager covering the goal, timeline, dependencies, risks, and resource needs so work is aligned before execution begins. Once approved, the planning phase turns the initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, milestones, and a definition of done.

Execution is managed through regular communication and disciplined tracking. Daily standups help identify progress and blockers, weekly team or PM syncs review status and decisions, and milestone or sprint demos confirm tangible progress. Ownership is clearly distributed across roles such as product managers, project managers, developers, QA, and stakeholders to support accountability and cross-functional alignment.

Risk and communication are core parts of the process. OctoAcme maintains a risk register, escalates blockers through defined paths, and uses structured stakeholder updates to keep teams informed. The organization emphasizes communication cadence, transparency, and responder ownership so issues are surfaced early and supported by clear escalation paths.

Quality and release controls are built into delivery. PRs are expected to include issue references and acceptance criteria, CI must validate tests and linting, and manual or automated verification helps confirm feature readiness. Releases require smoke testing, launch checks, rollback planning, and post-deployment verification. After significant milestones, the team reviews what worked well and what should improve, turning retrospectives into action items that strengthen future delivery.
